# Gilded Fate — Visual Style Guide

## Visual thesis

The Gilded Vault is beautiful because it is dangerous: charcoal stone, deep midnight blue, and restrained crimson are cut by precise seams of ancient gold. Gold is never ordinary trim; it signals magic, reward, power, or attention.

## Core palette

- Void Black `#080A10` — screen edges and deepest shadows
- Vault Charcoal `#171923` — surfaces and panels
- Midnight Blue `#10233B` — environmental depth
- Oath Crimson `#631F27` — Vanguard and danger accents
- Aged Gold `#9C7432` — inactive ornament
- Fate Gold `#F4B94C` — focus, interaction, Resonance
- Ivory `#E9DFC7` — primary readable copy

## Interface rules

1. Use negative space and thin architectural frames; never use default Unity controls in player-facing screens.
2. Reserve bright gold for hover, selection, rare rewards, and maximum Resonance.
3. Animate with deliberate ease-in/ease-out motion. Small interactions take 120–220 ms; screen transitions take 350–600 ms.
4. Body copy must remain readable at 1080p. Flavor may be subdued, rules text may not.
5. Every screen has three depth layers: atmosphere, architecture, and interactive UI.

## Motion language

- Idle: slow drift, breathing light, dust, and sparse motes.
- Hover: rise, warm edge light, slight scale, soft shadow.
- Confirm: quick inward compression followed by a gold release.
- Damage: directional impact, brief hit pause, then particles and numbers.
- Resonance: orbiting fragments accelerate as the meter fills; maximum produces one restrained full-screen gold pulse.

## Character accents

- Vanguard: steel, warm gold, ember orange, worn crimson.
- Hexer: black violet, magenta, cyan rune-light, porcelain white.
