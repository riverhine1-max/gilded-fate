# The Scholar's Mistake

Hexer · Fate Debt 1 · MEM_02 · MAJOR

The scholar's first comparison used two impressions of the same unfinished day. In one, a visitor arrived before sunset. In the other, the gate remained empty. After the traveler demonstrated his method, only the first impression could be summoned clearly. The scholar entered the result among his successes.

~~The traveler's successful prediction proved that the future had always been fixed.~~

**His intervention had removed the future that would have disproved the prediction.**

The recovered notes contain the second impression before the demonstration. Its absence afterward was treated as an error in the earlier calculation. Yet the comparison had changed while it was being tested. The scholar had accepted the surviving answer without accounting for the answer that could no longer object.

This was more than mistaking an intervention for observation. He had allowed the method to decide what counted as evidence of its own success. The traveler could be genuinely helpful and still make that test dishonest. A result remained; the grounds upon which it might be questioned had been lost.

The first mistake was not a failed calculation. It was accepting a test that could discard its own counterexample.
